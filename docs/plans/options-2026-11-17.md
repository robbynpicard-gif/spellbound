# TypeL: options report (2026-11-17)

Research into what successful games do, and options for art, characters, cosmetics,
environments and sound. **Nothing here is decided.** The sound rebuild (section 5)
is the one piece that was built and shipped (D-041, v0.14.0); everything else is a proposal.

Samples that go with this report:
- `art-styles-compare.png`: nine art treatments of the same Apprentice's Tower (A–I).
- `wizzy-music-journey.mp3`: 2½ minutes of the new dynamic score: menu → level → combo heat → ward stress → recovery → level-won finale → boss → boss finale.
- `wizzy-sfx-reel.mp3`: every new sound effect in order (listed in section 5).

## 1. What successful games do that we can borrow

| Game | What works | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Peggle** | Ten "Masters", each with a single power triggered by green pegs; every five levels is themed to a master. Over-the-top win (Ode to Joy, slow-mo, "EXTREME FEVER"). | One power per wizard, triggered by an in-level pickup. Each wizard owns a themed chapter. Our finale already copies the "ridiculous celebration" idea. |
| **Hades** | Weapon **aspects**: one weapon, several versions that play very differently. Heat (stackable difficulty) for replay. | Each wizard is an "aspect" of the same typing game. Later: unlockable alternate versions of each wizard ("variants") instead of upgrades. |
| **Balatro** | Every scoring event stacks animation + number roll + a note that rises in pitch. Sound is the reward. | Captures already climb the scale; bonus ticks climb faster. Add number roll-ups on the score plate (not built). |
| **Darkest Dungeon** | Hard edges, pooling blacks, everything old and worn; one consistent look the whole game follows. | Inky option (C). The lesson either way: pick one set of rules and make every asset obey them. |
| **Epistory** (typing) | Praised mainly for its distinctive papercraft look and music, but criticised for bright scenes hurting word legibility, and for little reason to replay after finishing. | Legibility first: whatever the style, scrolls stay high-contrast. Replay must come from wizards/scoring, not story. |
| **Typing Maniac** (the original) | Instant start, clear failure, high-score chase. | Keep "never any barrier to play". |

**Three gaps the comparison exposes in the current game:**
1. **Every run plays the same.** Peggle and Hades solve this with characters that change *how* you play. → Section 3.
2. **The score is a number, not a show.** Balatro rolls numbers up and rings on every increment. → Score plate roll-up + bonus tally (small job, recommended next).
3. **No reason to come back tomorrow** beyond your own best. → Daily Challenge + stat tracker + cosmetics (already agreed, not built).

## 2. Art style options

See `art-styles-compare.png`. All nine are the *same scene* run through different treatments, so they show palette, line and texture, not finished hand-made art. A real hand-drawn style means re-authoring every asset in that style; the samples help pick the direction before that work.

| | Style | Feel | Legibility for falling words | Cost to produce |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Refined pixel (current) | Retro, crisp, cosy-dark | Excellent | Low (generator exists) |
| B | Engraved / etched | Old book plate, scholarly | Good on dark scrolls | Medium |
| C | **Inky gothic** (Darkest Dungeon-like) | Heavy blacks, stark, dramatic | Good (lots of dark space) | Medium-high |
| D | Watercolour | Soft, melancholy | Fair (busy texture) | High |
| E | Gouache storybook | Warm, illustrated | Good | High |
| F | Charcoal & chalk | Gritty, sketchbook | Fair | Medium |
| G | Woodcut print | Bold, graphic, unusual | Poor behind text | Medium |
| H | Painterly | Rich, moody | Good | High |
| I | Cinematic | Lit, atmospheric | Good | High |

**Recommendation:** **C (inky)** or **B (engraved)** for the world, because both suit "the world forgetting itself": pages fading back to bare paper and ink draining away is a natural way to show the ward failing. Keep the falling scrolls and HUD crisp and high-contrast whichever style wins. A hybrid also works: pixel art for characters and UI, inked backdrops.

## 3. Characters (wizards)

Peggle model: **each wizard has one signature power** that changes the moment-to-moment game, plus a small passive. Powers trigger from a **sigil scroll** (a rare falling word in the wizard's colour, like Peggle's green peg). Type it and the power fires. All wizards share the same spells and levels, so balance stays manageable.

| Wizard | Passive | Signature power (sigil scroll) | How it changes the stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Apprentice** (default) | None | **Second Wind**: the next 5 words count double and can't break the combo | Candles flare; the room brightens |
| **The Archivist** | Long words fall 10% slower | **Recall**: for 8 s, the first 3 letters + Enter capture a word (if unique on screen) | Shelves pull words back into the books; page-dust drifts upward |
| **The Chronomancer** | Combo decays a tier instead of resetting on a miss | **Rewind**: undoes the last miss (ward damage and combo) | Clock gears in the walls run backwards; scrolls rise for a second |
| **The Pyromancer** | +20% score, ward takes +20% damage | **Kindle**: the next 4 captures explode, burning neighbours to half length | Hearth roars; embers rise; the window glows orange |
| **The Hedge Witch** | Spell scrolls drop more; mana capped lower | **Overgrowth**: vines catch the 3 lowest scrolls and hold them for 6 s | Roots break through the floor; moss creeps over the ward line |
| **The Warden** | Starts every level with one barrier block | **Bastion**: ward can't take damage for 6 s, but score is halved | Stone tiles rise; chains rattle; the light turns cold |
| **The Cartographer** *(new)* | Words spawn in lanes | **Compass**: all words slide to the centre lane for 5 s (easy sweeps) | Map lines draw themselves across the floor |
| **The Lexicant** *(new, silly)* | Typos are free (no Perfect lost) | **Malapropism**: every word on screen becomes a 3–4 letter word | The books start shouting; letters fall off spines |

**Questions:** keep the roster at 6, or add the two new ones? Signature power from a **sigil scroll** (Peggle-like, random), or a **meter that fills** with captures (more skill-based)?

### Wizard-themed environments
Each wizard gets a **home chapter** that reflects their power, so playing them *feels* different before you type a word:

- Apprentice → **the Tower study** (current)
- Archivist → **the Endless Stacks**: ladders, catalogue drawers, dust in shafts of light
- Chronomancer → **the Clock Loft**: giant gear faces, pendulum shadows sweeping the field
- Pyromancer → **the Kiln**: glassblower's forge, heat shimmer over the field
- Hedge Witch → **the Root Cellar**: hanging herbs, jars, roots through stone
- Warden → **the Gatehouse**: portcullis, braziers, rain on battlements

Options: (a) the wizard's home is their first chapter only, (b) **the whole campaign reskins to the chosen wizard** (most distinct, most art), (c) home stage is a separate "Trial" level per wizard.

## 4. Cosmetic progression (per wizard)

No paid items, no randomness, no loot boxes: everything is earned by playing that wizard, and the path is visible from the start.

Each wizard has a **mastery track (levels 1–20)** fed by score earned with them. Rewards alternate between five slots:

| Slot | Examples (Pyromancer) |
|---|---|
| **Robe** (palette + trim) | Ember, Soot, Molten, Ashen White |
| **Familiar** (sits on the desk, reacts to combos) | Salamander → Phoenix chick → Phoenix |
| **Typewriter** (the desk machine) | Brass → Blackened iron → Obsidian with lava seams |
| **Capture flourish** (what happens when a word is caught) | Puff of smoke → Embers → Firework |
| **Title** (shown on leaderboard) | "Kindler", "Ash-Speaker", "Who Set the Library on Fire" |

Plus **feat cosmetics** tied to skill, not time, for example: clear a level with no typos (golden typewriter keys); reach ×10 for 30 s (robe embers); win a boss without spells (title). And **a final "legend" look** at mastery 20 that changes the home environment, e.g. the Kiln becomes a cathedral of glass.

**Question:** one shared track for all wizards, or one track per wizard (deeper, slower)?

## 5. Sound design (built: D-041, v0.14.0)

### Principles
- **Everything in one key.** All effects are tuned to the score (D minor pentatonic), so they sit inside the music.
- **Rewards rise.** Each capture in a combo plays the next note up the scale; a miss resets it (Peggle and Balatro). Bonus ticks climb faster still.
- **Never the same twice.** Keys and captures vary slightly in pitch, level and timing every time.
- **Mistakes are soft.** A typo is a wooden knock and keeps your combo; a wrong word is crumpled paper and a sigh; only a real miss is harsh (stone crack + boom).
- **Spells sound like their element.** Fire: ignition, roar, crackle. Ice: freezing crackle, glass chimes, creak. Shock: electric buzz and zaps. Slow: tape-stop and slowing tick-tock. Barrier: rising hum and gong. Alter: reversed swell and page flips. Wind: gusts and whistle. Harvest: pages gathered and a rising chime.

### Dynamic score
Industry-standard design is vertical layering (stems fading in and out over a shared timeline) plus switching sections, with changes landing on beat or bar boundaries and stingers covering the seams. Ours does all three:
- **Eleven layers** (bed, arpeggio, heartbeat, clock tick, dissonance, wind, bells, choir, fever, ostinato, drums) always play in sync; game state only moves their volumes.
- **Two dials:** *stress* (ward instability) darkens the score: arpeggio notes start "forgetting", ticks jitter, dissonance and wind rise. *Heat* (combo tier) brightens it: bells, choir, then the fever layer.
- **Seamless transitions:** changes are queued and take effect **on the next bar line**, then glide over most of that bar. Rising stress lands with a swell; recovering plays a release chime.
- **Moods** (menu / level / boss) change tempo and chords on a bar line too.
- **Finale:** the score ducks under the typewriter bell, then choir, organ, timpani and bells (boss version in a darker key).

### Open sound questions
- Should **heat** add percussion (more energetic) or stay choral (more mystical)?
- Each wizard gets their own **leitmotif layer** (Peggle 2 gave each master a theme)? Recommended yes, once the roster is fixed.
- Real recorded typewriter samples later (needs licensed or self-recorded audio), or stay fully synthesised?
