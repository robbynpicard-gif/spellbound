## 1. Technical audit

### 1.1 Rendering and scenes (Phaser 3.90)
- **Screen and art scale:** the canvas is 1280×720 with `Scale.FIT` and `roundPixels: true`. All pixel art is authored at **640×360** and shown at **2×** (`ART_SCALE = 2`, nearest-neighbour filtering set in `BootScene`), so every art pixel is a 2×2 block on screen.
- **Scenes:** `BootScene` loads the art and fonts, `BackdropScene` sits behind the HTML menus, and `GameScene` plays one level.
  - **Important:** `GameScene` is restarted for every level (`scene.start`), and a "Level cleared" or reward screen sits between levels. Nothing visual survives from one level to the next today.
- **Layering in a level:** `FieldBackground` stacks everything by depth.
  - −10: the backdrop image.
  - −9.5: a darkening overlay that deepens through the chapter.
  - −9: the seal's damage shading and cracks.
  - −8.5: weather particles.
  - −8.4: the wizard image.
  - −8: the danger glow.
  - −7: the ward line.
  - −6: the shield.
  - 1 and 10: words (10 is the targeted word).
  - 19–21: effects.
  - 30–32: HUD.
  - 40: pop-ups.
- **Layout:** every position on screen comes from `src/render/layout.ts`.
  - Field: y 20 → ward line at 506, x 150–1076.
  - Paper roll: (472, 512, 256×60).
  - Counter: (772, 528, 128×44); combo plate: (772, 580, 128×36).
  - Rack: (1098, 126, 174×484).
  - Status bar: y 654.
  - Seal: centre (600, 500), radius 150.
  - Wizard: top-left (36, 388).
- **The HUD draws its own content** over "recesses" painted into the art: paper, counter wheels, combo, rack scrolls, status text and bars. Any new art must keep those recesses where `layout.ts` says they are, or move both together.
- **Gameplay is independent of art:** the simulation (`GameEngine`, fixed 60 Hz, seeded) knows nothing about art. Rendering reacts to engine events: `wordCompleted`, `wordMissed`, `typingError`, `spellCast`, `spellCollected`, `instabilityChanged` (with a stage from 0 to 4), `progressChanged`, `gameOver`. These are exactly the hooks character animations need. Duels replay on the server, so visuals must never feed back into the simulation.

### 1.2 Current character, typewriter and environment assets
- **Assets:**
  - `public/assets/art/backdrop-{ch1..ch4, boss1..boss4}.png`: one flat 640×360 image per chapter or boss, with the room, the view, the typewriter, the counter, the rack frame and the status frame all baked into it.
  - `wizard-<id>-<a|b>.png`: a 96×128 static sprite, drawn with shape primitives. This is the weakest asset: proportions read as cartoony.
  - `menu.png`, `library.png`: menu scenes.
- **Generator** (`tools/pixelart/v5/`, Python, Pillow + numpy):
  - `px.py`: canvas, masks, dithered gradients, noise, the `Sprite` material renderer and the colour grade.
  - `realistic.py`: the lighting model (flat colour × point lights, emissive parts with bloom, shading from a height field).
  - `interior.py`: reusable props (stone, books, candles, bottles, skull, owl, armillary sphere, seal, ornate frames, gems).
  - `scenes.py`: the outside views.
  - `wizards.py`: the shape-based wizards.
  - `build.py`: writes the PNGs.
- **Static only:** there is no animation anywhere. Candles, seal and gem glows are baked into the backdrop, and the only motion comes from code (particles, tweens, camera flashes).

### 1.3 Constraints
1. **All art is procedural.** Assets are generated by code in this sandbox: no image downloads, no external image models, no new Python or npm packages (the network is blocked). Quality depends on how much hand-specified detail goes into the generators.
2. **Pixel crispness:** keep 640×360 at 2× and nearest-neighbour scaling. Bloom is baked as soft gradients, so in the new work it should stay subtle, and glows that animate should be separate sprites.
3. **Light is baked:** lighting is computed in Python, not at runtime. "Environment lighting influences the character" therefore means either pre-lighting each character sheet per environment state, or tinting the sprite at runtime. I recommend pre-lit sheets per chapter plus small runtime tints for flashes (spell casts, ward damage).
4. **Level-to-level continuity:** the scene restarts each level, with a results screen in between. "Transitions without pausing gameplay" therefore has to happen **inside a level** (driven by level progress or boss time), or be a crossfade at the start of the next level. A transition across the level-clear screen is a pause by design.
5. **Readability:** words are parchment plaques at depth 1 and 10. The backdrop behind the field (y 20–506, x 150–1076) must stay low in contrast and detail. Animated layers there must be slow and dark.
6. **Brief vs. current layout:** the brief asks for character + typewriter at about 20–25% of screen height with 75–80% for play. Today the field ends at y 506 (70%), and the status bar takes y 654–708. In the concept, the anchor runs from the Apprentice's hood at about y 340 to the typewriter base at 636 (41%), with his head overlapping the bottom of the field, as Peggle's characters do. Getting closer to 25% means a smaller character, or a slimmer status bar with the ward line moved down. This is **a choice for review**.
7. **Testing:** I can render art and run the engine tests here, but I can't open the game in a browser. The Chrome extension isn't connected, and the full Phaser build isn't installed in the sandbox. Each milestone's art can be shown at actual scale as rendered images; the live result needs the owner's eyes.

### 1.4 Reuse vs. replace
| Keep / extend | Replace or rebuild |
|---|---|
| 640×360 @2× art pipeline, `px.py`, the lighting model and bloom in `realistic.py` | `wizards.py` shape wizards: a new character pipeline (hand-specified parts, height-field shading, animation frames) |
| Props in `interior.py` (stone, books, candles, bottles, seal, frames, gems) | The single baked backdrop per chapter: split into layers (see 2.1) |
| HUD frames (status bar, rack, counter), spell icons, `WordView` plaques, audio | `typewriter_front`: replaced by the character's signature typewriter (concept below) |
| `FieldBackground` effects (ward line, danger glow, shield, seal damage, weather) | Weather and darkening: become part of each environment's progression states |
| `layout.ts` as the single source of positions | — |

## 2. Proposed architecture (to approve before building)

### 2.1 Character worlds, as data
`src/data/worlds/apprentice.json` (a new file):
```json
{
  "character": "apprentice",
  "typewriter": "apprentice",
  "chapters": [
    {
      "id": "tower",
      "levels": [1, 5],
      "layers": [
        { "key": "tower-sky", "depth": -12, "parallax": 0.0 },
        { "key": "tower-room", "depth": -10 },
        { "key": "tower-fg", "depth": -8.2 }
      ],
      "ambient": ["candle-flicker", "dust", "clouds"],
      "states": [
        { "atProgress": 0, "level": 1, "tint": "dusk" },
        { "atProgress": 0.5, "level": 3, "tint": "night" }
      ]
    }
  ],
  "transition": { "seconds": 7 }
}
```
- Gameplay code never reads this file. Only rendering does.
- The wizard choice (from the roguelite plan) selects a world; the Apprentice is the first.

### 2.2 Rendering (new, small, separate classes)
- **`EnvironmentView`** replaces the single backdrop image in `FieldBackground`.
  - It stacks the layers, runs ambient animation (spritesheets or tweens), and crossfades between progression states in 5–10 seconds.
  - States are triggered by level progress, boss time or instability, so transitions happen during play, never between frames that matter. Words, the HUD and input are untouched.
- **`CharacterView`**: one spritesheet per character (and lighting state), with an animation state machine:
  - idle, typing, correct-word, combo, spell casting, ward damage, critical ward, victory, defeat.
  - Driven by the engine events listed in 1.1.
- **`TypewriterView`**: the character's signature machine as its own layer in front of the character, with small animations: keys or typebars on each keystroke, the carriage stepping, the ward crystal pulsing with the ward state.
- **`layout.ts`** gains `character` and `typewriter` anchors; the HUD positions stay where they are.

### 2.3 Asset pipeline
- `tools/pixelart/v5/worlds/apprentice/` will generate:
  - layer PNGs;
  - character spritesheets (frames in a strip, plus a JSON frame map);
  - the typewriter sheet.
- `BootScene` loads them from a manifest.
- The existing `build.py` keeps working for the other chapters until they are redone.

## 3. First concept (for review)

### The Apprentice
- An adult scholar in a deep, slightly peaked charcoal-plum hood.
- His face is mostly in shadow, with two faint eye glints and only the cheeks and nose catching candlelight.
- A long, rough grey beard spills over his chest.
- A leather strap with a brass buckle crosses his chest; a teal crystal pendant hangs on a cord.
- Worn leather gloves reach down to the keys, with a magenta rune ring on one.
- Rim light: magenta from the seal behind him and cool moonlight from the window.
- Sprite: 120×96 design units at 1.3× (156×125 art px), shown at 2×.

### Signature typewriter
- Seen from the far side and slightly above, so the keys face him.
- Visible from our side:
  - the fan of typebars and its brass segment;
  - ribbon spools, one magenta and one teal;
  - knurled brass platen knobs and the carriage-return lever;
  - key-tops peeking out either side of the paper;
  - a walnut body with brass edging and rivets;
  - an exposed gear train behind glass;
  - the magenta ward crystal on the front.
- The paper (the progress and typing console) rises from the platen toward the player.
- The score counter is bolted to its right side with a winding key.

### The Apprentice's Tower (chapter 1, base state)
- **The room:** a round stone room at the top of a tower.
  - Stone courses curve with the wall and darken toward the sides.
  - Roof beams and corbel posts; a candle chandelier; dried herbs hanging from a beam.
  - Left: a full-height bookshelf with a cubby of rolled scrolls, bottles and a ladder.
  - Right: a star chart pinned to the stone and a brass telescope aimed out of the window; a cobweb in the corner.
  - Desk: books, inkwell and quill, candles, an hourglass and vials.
  - The seal glows on the window glass behind the Apprentice.
- **The view** through a tall arched leaded window: a moonlit sky with thin clouds, three ranges of mountains that get hazier with distance, a forest and a few village lights in the valley, and mist.
- **Readability:** the field behind the words is mostly the dark window and wall, and the bright items (candles, chart, telescope) sit at the edges.

### Open questions for review
1. **Character size:** keep the current presence (anchor at about 41% with the head overlapping the field), or shrink it toward the brief's 20–25%?
2. **Typewriter orientation:** the concept keeps the keys facing the Apprentice. Is it clear enough that it's a typewriter? The alternative is a three-quarter view with the keys visible.
3. **Gloves:** they mostly sit behind the paper roll in the HUD. Show more of the hands (a narrower paper, or the hands moved outward)?
4. **The seal on the window glass behind him:** keep it, or move it to the wall or floor?
5. **Progression within the tower:** proposed as dusk at level 1 → night (2–3) → a storm gathering (4) → the boss with the tower under a blood moon (5). Approve the direction before the states are drawn.
