# Wizzy Type: full project handoff

_Snapshot: 2026-11-16, version 0.12.0. Level word counts are 5 lower than the table below (D-039)._

## 1. What it is
Wizzy Type is a browser typing game that reimagines the old web game *Typing Maniac*. A wizard sits in a gothic study at an enchanted typewriter, and word scrolls drift down past the study's arched windows. You type a word and press **Enter** to capture it before it reaches the window sill (the **ward line**). Each scroll that breaks through damages the ward (its "instability" rises). Some scrolls are **spell scrolls**: capturing one adds a spell charge, and typing the spell's name (e.g. `fire`) plus Enter casts it.

- **Repo:** github.com/robbynpicard-gif/spellbound (the repo name is still "spellbound"; the game is called Wizzy Type).
- **Live site:** https://robbynpicard-gif.github.io/spellbound/ (deployed by GitHub Actions on every push to `main`).
- **Owner:** has little programming knowledge. Changes are implemented in code, committed and pushed to `main`. Every decision is recorded in `docs/decisions.md` (D-001 … D-037).

## 2. Tech
- TypeScript 5.9, Phaser 3.90 (rendering), Vite (build), Vitest (tests). HTML/CSS menus over a Phaser backdrop scene.
- **Deterministic simulation**, separate from rendering: `GameEngine` runs at a fixed 60 Hz tick with a seeded RNG (Mulberry32) and keeps a command log for replay.
- **Multiplayer (Wizard Duels):** a Node + `ws` server in `server/` replays duels. It is not hosted yet. Both players share the same word list.
- **Saving:** `SaveManager` writes save version 4 to localStorage under the key `wizzytype.save`, falling back to the legacy key `spellbound.save`.
- **npm scripts:** `dev`, `build`, `test`, `typecheck`, `check`, `server`. CI runs the tests and the build, then deploys.
- **Art** is generated by Python scripts (Pillow + numpy) in `tools/pixelart/v5/`. Run `python3 tools/pixelart/v5/build.py` to write the images to `public/assets/art/`.

## 3. Gameplay rules (current)
- **Typing:** type the whole word, then press Enter. Matching ignores case. A typing console on the paper roll shows what you've typed.
- **Field:** the simulation is 1280 wide and 760 tall (the height went from 560 to 760 in D-036, so words fall further). Fall speed is in field pixels per second, and each word's speed varies ±25% around the level's speed. The pace rises over a level.
- **Words on screen:** `maxActiveWords` caps how many can fall at once (8 at level 1, rising to 15 at level 20). `spawnInterval` sets how often a new word appears.
- **Difficulty:** words get longer and more complex level by level. A word's complexity score counts rare letters (j, k, q, x, z = 1; v, w, y = 0.5), +1 for each run of three or more consonants, and +2 for a capitalised name. Each level can cap the maximum complexity.
- **Instability (ward):** each miss adds instability; at 100% the run is lost. The seal in the art cracks and dims as instability rises.
- **Campaign:** one run through 20 levels in 4 chapters, about 20 minutes. Levels 5, 10, 15 and 20 are survival bosses lasting 60, 75, 90 and 120 seconds, with the pace ramping up during each.
  - **Normal mode:** instability resets between levels.
  - **Hardcore mode:** instability carries over between levels. Clearing a boss heals 25.
  - **Boss reward:** after each boss, choose 1 of 3 rewards.
  - **Records:** saved separately for each mode.
- **Endless Practice:** play any unlocked level with no end.
- **Wizard Duels:** multiplayer mode (server not hosted).
- **A perfect typist** needs about 110+ raw WPM to clear level 20; the in-game WPM readout shows about 58.

## 3b. Audio (D-038)
All sound is synthesised in `src/audio/Audio.ts` (Web Audio API, no files). Music: dark D-minor ambient loop (pad, drone, plucked arpeggio, bells, reverb), denser in boss fights. Sound effects: key ticks, capture chime rising with combo, error buzz, miss thud, barrier ring, spell pickup sparkle, a voice per spell cast, level fanfare, defeat. Music and sound effects are separate switches in Options and the pause menu, saved in `settings: { music, sfx }`.

## 4. Spells (`src/data/spells.json`)
There are 8 spells, listed below in power order and introduced in this order. All runs share one spell pool (the "mana capacity"), with no per-spell maximum; rarer spells simply drop less often. On the level that introduces a spell, that spell drops more often (`INTRO_DROP_BOOST`). Cast a spell by typing its name plus Enter. No falling word can equal a spell name, start with one, or be the start of one.

| Spell | Effect | Drop weight |
|---|---|---|
| slow | Words fall slower for a while (stacks ×0.6 each; paused while frozen) | 5 |
| ice | Freezes every word for 5 s (stacks) | 4 |
| barrier | Blocks the next 2 misses | 4 |
| shock | Zaps the lowest word and the 3 nearest | 3 |
| alter | Long words become short for good; then 8 s of short, easy new words | 3 |
| fire | Burns every word away (no points); introduced first as a bailout | 2 |
| wind | Restores your ward (in Hardcore, halves instability) | 1.5 |
| harvest | Claims every word on screen at half points (counts toward progress) | 1 |

Crash was removed. When a level introduces a spell, a pop-up shows its one-line summary before the level starts.

## 5. Words (`src/data/words.json`, D-036)
- One combined dictionary of **2,910 words**: medieval life, wizardry, alchemy, nature and everyday words, plus names from public-domain myth, folklore, Arthurian legend and Shakespeare.
- No brand names and no names from modern fiction (a test checks this).
- Lengths are 3–12 letters, letters only; only legend names are capitalised.
- Spell names are excluded.
- The old Fantasy/Modern word banks were removed; Options now holds the audio switches.

## 7. Art direction (current: D-037)
- **Style:** dark, gritty gothic pixel art. Realistic lighting, not cartoony. The owner's reference image sets the mood: a gothic library with stone arches, crowded bookshelves, candles, a skull, bottles, a magenta banner, a magenta rune seal, an ornate brass typewriter with a rolling score counter, an owl, an armillary sphere, and gem-studded brass HUD frames. Words fall on parchment plaques with brass studs.
- **Palette:**
  - Midnight `#0b0a10`
  - Magenta (arcane) `#b4409c`, with glow `#e25cc4`
  - Teal `#3aa49a`
  - Candle gold `#e6a03c`
  - Parchment `#e6d6b4`
- **Spell colours:**
  - Slow: magenta
  - Ice: teal
  - Barrier: silver-blue `#9aa4b8`
  - Fire: burnt orange `#d2692e`
  - Wind: green `#6a9a5a`
  - Shock: gold `#c8a640`
  - Alter: rose `#c07890`
  - Harvest: aged gold `#b48c48`
- **Fonts:** headings in Pixelify Sans; gameplay text in Atkinson Hyperlegible.
- **Rendering:** art is 640×360, shown at 2× on a 1280×720 screen. Scenes are drawn as flat colour multiplied by the light from every candle, the seal, glowing glass and the moon, then softened with bloom, textured, and given a gritty colour grade with no outlines. The wizard is shaded as a rounded form, rim-lit by the seal, with his face in shadow under the hat.
- **The study:** the same room in every chapter; the view through its two arched windows changes per chapter and boss, and the room's light colour follows it. Weather and darkness build level by level within a chapter: motes, bubbles, embers, or rain with lightning.
- **Menus:**
  - Main menu: a tower on a sea cliff, with pages drifting to its lit window.
  - Wizard select (when built): a candlelit library.
- **Spell rack (right side):** each scroll is fully coloured in its spell's colour, with a big symbol, the name and the charge count. Empty scrolls show desaturated.
- **Ward feedback:** a dashed rune line at the sill, a red danger glow, and the seal cracking.

## 8. History of the art (so it isn't repeated)
1. 16-bit 320×180 at 4×, drawn in code. Became darker and grittier ("original Fallout" feel).
2. Gothic spec plus a reference image, built in using the reference image itself as the backdrop (D-031). Rejected: one fixed image with small variations.
3. v2 to v3 original scenes per chapter (darker, less cartoony), with the wizard behind the typewriter.
4. v4: the reference's mood and detail, longer fall.
5. v5 / current (D-037): realistic lighting, zoomed-out room, reference positioning (wizard lower left, typewriter bottom centre facing the player).

**Owner's ongoing wishes:** more realistic and gritty, less childish or cartoony; capture the reference's details and mood; keep the layout zoomed out with plenty of vertical falling space.
