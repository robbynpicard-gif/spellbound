# WIZZY TYPE — VISUAL ART DIRECTION

> Status: proposed (2026-11-16), not yet built. Reference: `mockups/gothic-reference.png`; in-code pixel mockup: `mockups/gothic-play-screen.png`. Planned at 640×360 shown 2×. The source text ends partway through section 10.
**Visual Reference & Design Specification**

## 1. Overall Art Direction

Wizzy Type should use **gritty, dark gothic fantasy pixel art**.

The atmosphere should feel mysterious, aged, atmospheric and slightly ominous. Think of a dark medieval fantasy RPG combined with a carefully crafted pixel-art indie game.

The visual style should be:
- Refined, detailed pixel art.
- Dark, gritty and atmospheric.
- Weathered rather than polished.
- Mystical without becoming overly colourful or cartoonish.
- Visually rich, but always readable.
- Consistent in pixel scale, lighting and texture.

Avoid generic cartoon fantasy aesthetics, overly clean environments, excessive visual effects and bright, saturated colours across the entire scene.

## 2. Colour Palette

We have established a specific colour direction.

The most recent change is to move away from conventional purple and blue magical effects, replacing them with muted magenta and teal.

### Primary colours

**Magenta — Primary Arcane Magic**
- Used for the magical ward, runic seals, arcane energy and spell effects.
- Should feel mysterious and supernatural.
- Use muted, deep magenta as the base, with brighter pink-magenta highlights for active magic.
- Avoid excessive neon pink.

**Teal — Secondary Magical Colour**
- Used for ice-related magic, cool illumination, magical accents and environmental lighting.
- Use muted teal and blue-green tones.
- Avoid conventional bright cyan or electric blue.

**Warm Amber / Gold**
- Used for candlelight, brass, parchment, typewriter details and warm environmental lighting.
- Provides contrast against the cooler magical colours.

**Environment**
- Charcoal, deep blue-black, dark stone grey, weathered brown and aged wood.
- Keep the environment relatively desaturated.

### Colour philosophy

The environment should remain muted and gritty. Magical elements should stand out through controlled colour contrast and illumination.

Do not make the entire game colourful. Magenta and teal should be reserved for meaningful magical elements.

## 3. Environment

The primary setting is a dark, ancient wizard's library or study.

Visual characteristics:
- Gothic stone architecture.
- Weathered stone walls and floors.
- Dark, aged wooden bookshelves.
- Old books, manuscripts and alchemical equipment.
- Brass instruments and mechanical objects.
- Candles and localized warm lighting.
- Moonlight entering through gothic windows.
- Atmospheric shadows, subtle mist and environmental depth.

The environment should feel inhabited and old, not like a clean fantasy-themed room.

Texture is important, but avoid excessive dithering, grain or visual noise. Individual objects must remain distinguishable.

## 4. Wizard Character

The wizard should look like a weathered inhabitant of this world.

Avoid friendly, cartoon-like wizard designs.

Preferred characteristics:
- Hooded, shadowed appearance.
- Aged and weathered clothing.
- Dark robes and worn fabrics.
- Subdued facial features.
- Mysterious, serious presence.
- Natural proportions within the pixel-art style.

The wizard should feel like a character from a gritty medieval fantasy RPG rather than a mascot.

## 5. Enchanted Typewriter

The typewriter is the visual centrepiece of the gameplay environment.

It should look like an old, substantial mechanical instrument that has been enchanted.

Visual characteristics:
- Dark, aged wood and tarnished brass.
- Detailed mechanical keys.
- Physical parchment feeding through the machine.
- Subtle magical illumination.
- Weathered surfaces and mechanical detail.

The typewriter should feel physical and believable, while clearly communicating its magical purpose.

### Score counter

Integrate a brass mechanical number counter into the typewriter.

- Physical rolling number wheels.
- Dark recessed housing.
- Aged brass frame.
- Clear, high-contrast digits.
- Subtle golden illumination when the score increases.

The score should be easy to read without dominating the typewriter.

## 6. Ward — Glowing Runic Seal

Represent ward health through a large magical runic seal integrated into the stone wall behind the typewriter.

Visual characteristics:
- Circular arrangement of ancient runes.
- Central magical symbol.
- Magenta magical illumination.
- Subtle floating particles and energy.
- Weathered stone surroundings.

The seal should visibly deteriorate as ward health decreases.

Suggested visual states:
- High health: Complete, bright runes and stable magenta glow.
- Medium health: Flickering runes and reduced illumination.
- Low health: Visible fractures, unstable energy and darker magenta/red highlights.
- Depleted: Seal fractures and protective magic collapses.

The seal communicates health visually, but exact ward health must remain available through a clear numerical indicator and progress bar.

## 7. Round Progress — Advancing Enchanted Parchment

Round progress should be physically integrated into the typewriter through an enchanted parchment roll.

- Parchment mounted through brass rollers above the keyboard.
- Aged, slightly distressed parchment.
- Completed words represented by illuminated inscriptions or filled segments.
- Remaining words represented by empty segments.
- Parchment physically advances as words are completed.

The progress indicator must remain immediately understandable.

Always retain:
- A clearly segmented progress bar.
- Exact completed/total word count.
- Strong visual distinction between completed and remaining progress.

The physical parchment is the visual representation; the progress bar and numerical count provide precision.

## 8. Spell Inventory — Vertical Coloured Scroll Rack

The spell inventory should be a vertical rack of physical parchment scrolls positioned on the right side of the gameplay screen.

This is an important and defining feature of Wizzy Type.

### Scroll design

- The entire scroll should be coloured according to its spell.
- Spell colour must be immediately recognizable from peripheral vision.
- Use a large, simple central symbol.
- Display the spell name clearly.
- Display remaining charges prominently.
- Use minimal decorative detail.
- Preserve a parchment/scroll appearance.

The scrolls should be simplified compared with traditional ornate fantasy scrolls.

**The spell colour must dominate the entire scroll, not just a small ribbon or decorative accent.**

### Spell colour identities

Maintain consistent spell colours across the entire game.

Examples:
- Slow: Muted magenta/purple.
- Ice: Muted teal.
- Barrier: Desaturated silver-blue.
- Fire: Burnt orange.
- Wind: Muted green.
- Shock: Muted golden yellow.
- Alter: Muted rose.
- Harvest: Aged gold.

The exact shades can be refined, but each spell must have a distinct, consistent colour identity.

### Scroll states

- Available: Full spell colour.
- Selected: Illuminated border or subtle magical glow.
- Activated: Brief unfurling animation and magical effect.
- Depleted: Desaturated but still recognizable.

Avoid excessive ornamentation or complicated scroll illustrations.

## 9. Gameplay Interface and Readability

The game should feel immersive, but gameplay information must never become difficult to understand.

Use a hybrid approach: physical objects communicate information through the environment, while clear interface indicators provide exact values.

Maintain:
- Clear progress bars.
- Exact numerical values.
- High-contrast falling words.
- Clearly highlighted targeted words.
- Readable spell names and charge counts.
- Distinct visual separation between gameplay elements and background scenery.

The visual environment must never compete with falling words.

## 10. Pixel-Art Quality Standards

Establish consistent visual production standards:

- Consistent pixel scale across sprites and environments.
- Crisp pixel edges.
- Avoid fractional scaling and blurry interpolation.
- Use deliberate pixel clusters rather than excessive dithering.
- Use outlines and contrast
